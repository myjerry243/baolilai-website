Automotive & parts

Parts that fail in service, not in the showroom.

Automotive coating is a specification business: salt spray, stone chip, detergent, UV, and a colour that has to match a master panel three years later. We supply the five chemistries that cover the part list below, with the test data attached.

Engine components

Heat and oil resistance, tight tolerances

Aluminium wheels

Class-A face, stone-chip resistance, UV

Suspension springs

Flexibility plus impact — coating must bend with the part

Chassis & underbody

Salt spray 1000 h, gravel impact

Filters

Chemical resistance, thin uniform film

Control levers

Wear resistance, grip feel

Mirrors & trim

Colour match, gloss consistency

Wiper arms

Detergent and UV resistance

Horns & small parts

Edge coverage on complex shapes

Five chemistries

Pick the chemistry by where the part lives.

ChemistryWhere it belongs
EpoxyBest corrosion and chemical resistance; interior parts only
Epoxy-polyesterBalance of cost and appearance; interior and sheltered
PolyesterExterior durability, UV and gloss retention

AcrylicHighest gloss retention and clarity

The same part can need two different systems depending on whether it sees road salt or engine heat. Send the drawing and the service condition, not just the part name.

What gets tested

The five numbers a tier-one buyer asks for.

Corrosion

Neutral salt spray 500–1000 h depending on part class and film build.

Stone chip

Gravel impact per the OEM standard the part is released against.

Chemical

Engine oil, brake fluid, coolant, detergent — each with a method and a time.

Appearance

Gloss, DOI and ΔE against the approved master panel.

We are not IATF 16949 certified today. If a programme requires it, we will say so at the quoting stage rather than after the audit. For aftermarket and non-critical parts, PPAP-level documentation can be discussed case by case.
