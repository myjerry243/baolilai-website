Technology

Three process systems that decide the finish.

Formulation is half the product. How the pigment is bonded, how the melt is extruded and how the fine fraction is separated decide whether a specification holds on a production line.

Bonding — metallic effects that survive handling

Metallic pigment is fixed onto the base particle instead of being dry-blended. The ratio stays constant through transport, spraying and reclaim.

Low-temperature extrusion — 130 °C without losing the film

A dedicated extrusion route for low-cure chemistry. It opens MDF, thin-wall and heat-sensitive assemblies that a standard system would distort or char.

Ultrafine separation — the fraction you do not want

Particle size distribution drives transfer efficiency and surface quality. A cyclone separation system (patent CN223010819U) controls the fine fraction.

Quality control — measured, not asserted

Gel time, particle size, film build, gloss, colour, adhesion, impact and salt spray are checked against the specification, with a retained sample for twelve months.

Application principle

Corona charging, then a hot oven.

Powder is fluidised by compressed air, pushed through a gun with a high-voltage electrode at the tip. Corona discharge charges the particles; the earthed workpiece pulls them in. The part then enters the oven, where the powder melts, flows and cures into a continuous film.

Typical settings: 60–90 kV, gun-to-part 150–250 mm, film build 60–90 µm in one pass. Overspray is recovered and blended back — total utilisation above 95%, against 40–60% for liquid paint.

Why this matters commercially: the customer does not buy kilos, they buy square metres of finished part.
